Rather than outright rejection, they frame AI as an unrefined tool that paradoxically encourages more daring creative approaches. Wintory notes that legal copyright issues make AI-generated music a "non-starter" for most publishers, with recent contracts explicitly banning AI-created work. The composers argue their value lies not in the final music product but in the collaborative creative process—a dimension AI cannot replicate. Meanwhile, the rise of streaming, vinyl releases, and sold-out concerts has elevated game music from background utility to standalone art form. This cultural shift, combined with AI's legal and creative limitations, suggests human composers will remain essential partners in game development rather than being replaced by algorithms.
